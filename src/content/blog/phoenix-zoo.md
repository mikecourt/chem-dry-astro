### Africa Trail

Nestled in Papago Park, the Phoenix Zoo is a wondrous place of curiosity, imagination, and discovery. As one of the largest non-profit zoos in the country, it offers miles of trails through natural habitats and special experiences like the Red Barn Petting Zoo, Stingray Bay, and camel rides.

A major draw is the Africa Trail, which showcases animals from across the world. The Desert Lives trail diverts from the Africa Trail just before and after the lion and hyena exhibit, with desert bighorn sheep and Arabian oryx roaming among the natural buttes.

The zoo gained worldwide attention in 1973 when an Asian elephant named Ruby began painting, creating works of art that were sold for hundreds of dollars each. Today, you can become a “piece of the zoo” by purchasing your own brick in Predator Passage.

### Tropics Trail

The Tropics Trail features exotic animals from rainforests around the world. It also includes the Children’s Trail and a petting zoo.

During the early years of the Phoenix Zoo, financial struggles threatened to derail the project. This was when Nancy Maytag stepped in to make her husband’s dream a reality.

During the zoo’s most recent renovation, they added a Children’s area that includes a petting zoo. They also renovated part of the park to include a new exhibit called “Ruby’s House” named after the painting elephant at the zoo. This exhibit teaches visitors about conservation through art. The Phoenix Zoo also provides sanctuary to animals that cannot be returned to the wild due to age, behavioral issues, or injuries. ### Children’s Land

Children will want to spend a lot of time at the Children’s Trail. They can pet animals at the Red Barn, play in Discovery Farm, or say hello to Fernando, a two-toed sloth. The area also has a treehouse and slides.

The Phoenix Zoo is home to many animals that are in the sanctuary. These animals may participate in captive breeding programs, have retired from such programs, or need a place to live because they cannot return to the wild for various reasons.

The best time to visit the zoo is in the winter, spring, or fall. This is when the temperatures are much cooler and it is more enjoyable for both humans and animals. There are special discounts that can be found in brochures that will usually include a voucher for free admission to the zoo.

### Sea Life Walk

Visiting zoos is a favorite pastime for many people. People love to see exotic animals in their natural habitats, and they also learn about various species and their habits. They also enjoy recreational activities, such as rides and petting zoos. Zoos also play a role in conservation efforts.

The Phoenix Zoo is one of the oldest zoos in the United States, and it has gained a reputation for animal advocacy. It has four trails that showcase animals in their natural environments, and it also provides educational experiences.

The Zoo offers many unique experiences, including Stingray Bay (where you can pet a string ray), the Endangered Species Carousel, camel rides, and giraffe feeding. It also has many gift shops and concession stands. The zoo also provides sensory bags for visitors with special needs.

### Desert Trail

Although it’s not one of the largest zoos in the country, Phoenix Zoo is still a must-visit for visitors to Arizona. The Zoo provides a home to thousands of animals and features multiple unique habitats that replicate natural desert settings.

The Arizona Trail is designed to resemble the wildlife and plants native to the state, including saguaro cacti, coyote, fennec fox, Sonoran pronghorn, coyote, mountain lion, turkey vulture, thick-billed parrot, and javelina. The exhibits are spread out throughout the entire area and have a very natural feel.

You should allow at least 3-4 hours to fully experience Phoenix Zoo and its 125 acres of natural habitats.
