If you've lived in Chicago for more than one winter, you know what happens to carpets. Slush tracked in from the L. Mud from Millennium Park after a rainy afternoon. Salt stains ground into the fibers near every entryway. When it's finally time to clean, Chicago homeowners face a choice: the same old steam cleaning everyone's used for decades, or something that actually works better.

More and more Chicago families are switching to Hot Carbonating Extraction (HCE) — and once you understand how it works, it's easy to see why.

## What Is Hot Carbonating Extraction?

Hot Carbonating Extraction is the proprietary carpet cleaning process used by Chem-Dry. Instead of flooding carpets with hot water and detergent the way traditional steam cleaning does, HCE uses the natural power of carbonation to lift dirt and grime to the surface, where it can be extracted cleanly.

The process uses **The Natural®**, a carbonated cleaning solution that is green-certified and safe for your family and pets. Millions of tiny bubbles penetrate deep into carpet fibers, breaking the bond between dirt and the fiber itself. A hot carbonating extraction machine then pulls that loosened soil out of the carpet.

The result: a deeper clean with dramatically less water.

## The Problem With Steam Cleaning in Chicago Homes

Steam cleaning — also called hot water extraction — works by saturating carpets with water and then extracting as much of it as possible. The operative phrase is "as much of it as possible," because steam cleaning leaves behind a significant amount of moisture.

In Chicago's humid summers and cold winters, that moisture creates real problems:

**1. Long drying times.** Steam-cleaned carpets can take 24–48 hours to fully dry. In a Chicago home, that means a day or two of walking on damp carpets, keeping pets off the area, and hoping the weather cooperates. When it doesn't, you risk mold or mildew growth beneath the surface — invisible but damaging.

**2. Dirt-attracting residue.** Many steam cleaning methods use soaps and detergents that leave a residue in carpet fibers. That residue acts like a magnet for future dirt. You may notice that freshly steam-cleaned carpets look grimy again within weeks — not because your family is dirtier than average, but because the cleaning solution itself is drawing in new soil.

**3. Over-wetting risks.** Excessive moisture can soak through carpet backing and into the subfloor, causing structural damage, warping, and long-term odor issues that are costly to remediate.

## How HCE Solves These Problems

The Chem-Dry HCE process uses **about 80% less water** than typical steam cleaning. That's not a marketing claim — it's a meaningful difference that changes the entire post-cleaning experience.

With significantly less moisture introduced, carpets cleaned with HCE dry in **1 to 2 hours** under normal conditions — not 1 to 2 days. You can have company over the same afternoon. Your kids and pets are back on the floor within hours.

Because The Natural® is a carbonated solution rather than a soapy detergent, it leaves **no dirt-attracting residue** behind. The carbonation does the work of lifting soil, not surfactants that cling to fibers. Your carpets stay cleaner longer.

And because HCE uses so much less water, there's no risk of over-wetting — protecting your floors, your subfloor, and the structural integrity of your home.

Chicago homes face specific challenges that make carpet hygiene especially important. Allergens like dust mites, pet dander, and mold spores accumulate in carpet fibers year-round. Outdoor pollutants come inside on shoes. In a city of this size, indoor air quality matters.

The Natural® is certified by the Carpet and Rug Institute (CRI) and has earned the Green Certified seal, meeting strict health and environmental standards. It's safe for use around children and pets — a priority for Chicago families who want a clean home without introducing harsh chemicals into their living spaces.

Independent studies have shown that the HCE process removes an average of **98.1% of common allergens** from carpets and upholstery. For families with allergies, asthma, or young children who spend time on the floor, that's a meaningful number.
